Common Summer Sports Injuries in Bernalillo Families

With more hours outside and on the field, certain injuries show up again and again. Some happen all at once; others build slowly over time.

Common summer injuries in active kids and adults include:

  • Sprained ankles from trail runs, basketball, or stepping wrong on uneven ground
  • Shoulder strains from swimming laps, throwing, or playing tennis and pickleball
  • Knee pain from soccer, running, and hikes on steep or rocky trails
  • Wrist or hand strains from falls or catching yourself the wrong way

Overuse injuries often creep up as the season goes on. At first, they feel like a small ache; then they start to affect performance or daily life. These can include:

  • Tendonitis in the elbows or shoulders from repeated throwing or swinging
  • Shin splints from running on hard surfaces or increasing mileage too quickly
  • Low back pain from repetitive motions, poor form, or sitting too much between games

Heat and dehydration can make all of this worse. When the body is tired or low on fluids, muscles do not respond as well. This can cause tightness, slower reaction times, and a higher chance of strains, pulls, or joint irritation, especially when kids jump from a mostly indoor school year into full-speed summer sports.

Preventing Summer Setbacks with Smart Movement Habits

Good habits go a long way toward preventing injuries in the first place. They also help the body recover better between practices, games, and outdoor adventures.

Helpful tips for active families include:

  • Warm up with light movement instead of jumping straight into hard play
  • Cool down with easy walking and gentle stretching
  • Increase training time or intensity slowly rather than all at once
  • Build in regular rest days so muscles and joints can recover

The right gear also matters. Supportive footwear that fits well can reduce stress on the ankles, knees, hips, and low back. Paying attention to posture during sports and daily activities, like carrying backpacks or lifting coolers, can reduce extra strain on the spine and shoulders.

Safe technique is another big piece. Good form in running, jumping, throwing, or swinging helps spread the work across the whole body instead of overloading one area. Coaching, practice, and body awareness all play a role here.

Periodic chiropractic checkups can help catch small imbalances early. When we notice areas that are tight, weak, or not moving well, we can address them before they lead to pain or limit performance. This supports athletes of all ages in staying aligned, feeling better, and playing their best all summer long and beyond.
